Check where the moving truck can load before you book a tight building window. The closest curb may have street-cleaning restrictions, a short time limit, or a driveway that must stay clear. The building's preferred entrance may also be on a different street from its mailing address.

Plan pickup and delivery separately. Photograph the proposed loading locations so the moving team can see the access conditions you are describing.

## Establish who controls the space

A building manager can explain private loading docks, garages, and driveways. Street parking is a different question. Identify whether the address is in the City of Los Angeles, another city, or an unincorporated part of the county before looking for parking instructions.

A Los Angeles postal address does not answer every jurisdiction question. Culver City, Santa Monica, West Hollywood, and other nearby cities have their own contacts and procedures. Use the authority responsible for the actual street.

Ask management which loading entrance is available and whether it must be reserved. If there is a dock, find out its clearance and whether another tenant or delivery company will use it during your move. Share those answers with the mover rather than assuming that a dock means guaranteed truck access.

## Read the signs at the loading point

Check the permanent signs, any temporary signs, curb markings, meter restrictions, and posted times. Read the whole group of signs that applies to the space. A photograph from the opposite side of the street may miss a restriction at the intended loading position.

Look at the actual moving day and hours. A place that works when you visit in the evening may be unavailable when the truck arrives on a weekday morning. LADOT's [parking tips](https://ladotparking.org/5-tips-avoid-citations/) explain why both permanent and temporary signs need attention.

If you are unsure about a restriction, ask the relevant parking authority before relying on the space. For City of Los Angeles contacts, start with [LADOT](https://ladot.lacity.gov/contact). Confirm the current procedure, any charges, and the timing of any temporary-sign request directly. Procedures and lead times can change.

Do not treat cones, a handwritten note, or a neighbor's promise as confirmation that public curb space has been reserved for your truck.

## Send the mover useful access information

Splendid lists a 26-foot moving truck. Ask the team for the vehicle's overall dimensions before requesting a space; the box size alone does not tell you the full vehicle length. The driver also needs to assess the approach, turning room, clearance, and stopping position. Tell the team about a narrow lane, steep driveway, low garage, overhead obstruction, or gate.

Photograph the street from the approach as well as the loading point. Include any height or access sign. If the building entrance faces another street, mark it on a simple screenshot or written note.

Measure or describe the carry from the loading position to your door. A move with a parking space directly beside the entrance takes a different route from a move that crosses a garage and several corridors. Those details help with crew and time planning.

Agree who will handle any building booking or parking request. Do not assume permit handling is included in the moving service unless the team confirms it for your job.

## Match the loading plan to the elevator plan

Confirm when the loading entrance opens, who can open it, and when the elevator reservation begins. Give the mover a contact who can answer during the moving window. A permit or a dock booking does not replace the building's separate moving requirements.

If the site also requires insurance paperwork, use the [COI and elevator checklist](/blog/moving-building-coi-elevator-reservations) to keep the confirmations together. Share changes with both the building and the mover as soon as you know about them.

## Keep a workable backup

Ask the team what happens if the expected loading point is unavailable. An alternative can change the carry distance, timing, or equipment needed. Review the options before the morning of the move.

Recheck the signs and access shortly before moving day, then confirm the arrangement with the building contact. When you [request a quote](/quote), send the loading photos with both addresses and your inventory. The [local moving team](/local-moving) can then review the route between the truck and the furniture.
