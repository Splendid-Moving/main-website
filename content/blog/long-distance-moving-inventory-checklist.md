A long-distance moving inventory should let someone understand the shipment without standing in your living room. List the furniture, account for storage areas, and photograph pieces whose size or handling needs are difficult to describe.

Send the same inventory to each mover you compare. If one company is pricing a full garage and another has only seen the bedroom furniture, their numbers will not be measuring the same job.

## Work through one room at a time

Start a list for each room and keep the names consistent. Record the large pieces first, then estimate the packed boxes. Add a final section for the balcony, garage, storage cage, or off-site storage unit.

Describe furniture in terms someone else can recognize. “Three-seat sofa with chaise” is more useful than “couch.” State the bed size and whether it has storage drawers. For a dining set, count the chairs and note a glass or stone top.

Flag items that are unusually heavy, fragile, valuable, or awkward to handle. Include dimensions when the size changes the plan. Tell the team what needs disassembly and if you have the assembly instructions.

Your first list may be approximate. Mark uncertain box counts and update them as packing progresses. An honest range is better than an exact number that leaves out half the cupboards.

## Take photos that answer a question

Use wide photos to show the room, then take closer photos of bulky or unusual items. Show the whole piece, including its base. Add a scale reference or measurements when the dimensions are hard to judge.

Photograph existing marks or damage on important pieces for your own records. Save those originals somewhere you can still reach during the move. A photo sent for pricing and a condition record serve different purposes; keep your own copies of both.

Check closets, wall-mounted items, and outdoor areas before calling the inventory finished. A television may need dismounting and protection. Splendid can discuss dismounting, wrapping, and transport, but does not offer mounting at the new address.

## Include access at pickup and delivery

Write down the complete addresses and contacts. Add floor numbers, stair flights, elevator availability, building move windows, and the distance from a usable truck loading point to the door.

Tell the team about narrow roads, gates, garage-height restrictions, or a destination that has not been confirmed yet. Do not guess that a truck can use an entrance simply because a passenger car can.

If you still need a lease, key release, or closing date, explain which dates are firm. Ask how pickup and delivery timing will be described in the written agreement. Keep the person receiving the shipment informed of any change.

## Keep documents and personal essentials with you

Keep identification, medication, keys, valuables, and the items needed during travel out of the shipment. Put moving paperwork and contact information where you can reach them without opening a packed box.

For an interstate move, FMCSA recommends checking registration, reviewing the moving documents, and taking part in the inventory review at pickup and delivery. Check the inventory against what is present and note questions about condition or missing items before signing. Its [moving checklist](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/moving-checklist) is a useful companion to your own room list.

California's [BHGS consumer guide](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/consumers/hhm.html) also explains the estimate and moving-agreement process. Ask which documents and rules apply to your route before comparing the prices and terms.

## Update the list before the load changes

Tell the mover if you add furniture, decide to move an appliance, or substantially change the packing scope. Ask how the revised inventory affects the written estimate and moving agreement before loading.
