Open a kitchen drawer and it is easy to find another packing task. Glasses need wrapping, pans fit badly in boxes, and the coffee maker still has to work tomorrow morning. Start by deciding what you will use until the move and what can be packed now.

Set out a small everyday kit. Keep enough dishes for the people in your home, one pan, basic utensils, and whatever you need for breakfast. Pack the rest by cupboard or drawer so the job has a clear stopping point.

## Use smaller boxes for the heavy contents

Dishes, cans, and jars get heavy quickly. Put them in boxes that can be lifted and carried safely. Save larger boxes for lighter plastic containers or other bulky items.

Check each box before filling it. A softened bottom or a damaged seam is a poor place for a set of plates. Tape the base securely and create a cushioned layer with suitable packing material. Keep wrapping material nearby so you do not have to balance partly wrapped dishes on the counter.

Avoid using clothing you will need immediately as the only packing material. It can leave the first night at the new place dependent on finding a particular kitchen box.

## Give each fragile item its own space

Wrap plates individually and place them on edge in a suitable box, with cushioning between pieces and around the sides. Pack glasses so they cannot knock together. Stems, handles, and lids need particular attention.

The Home Depot’s [dish-packing guide](https://www.homedepot.com/c/ah/how-to-pack-dishes-and-glasses/9ba683603be9fa5395fab901a8166d14) includes photos of the wrapping and on-edge packing method.

Fill empty space so the contents cannot shift freely. The goal is a supported package, not a box squeezed shut over too many items. If you can hear pieces knocking together when the box is moved gently, open it and adjust the packing.

Mark fragile boxes clearly and describe the contents. “Kitchen: wine glasses” tells you more than “fragile.” Write on a side as well as the top, and avoid putting something heavy on top while the boxes wait for pickup.

## Deal with food before moving day

Use up food you do not want to transport. Check pantry shelves for leaking containers and items past their useful date. Keep food separate from cleaning products.

Ask the moving team what food and liquids it will accept for your particular move. A local trip and a long-distance shipment create different storage conditions. Plan to carry temperature-sensitive food yourself when appropriate, and work out what you will do with it when you arrive.

Keep cooking oils and opened bottles upright in a container suited to catching a small leak. Confirm they are acceptable to move before including them in the truck inventory. Do not pack hazardous household chemicals with dishes or food.

## Photograph small-appliance parts before separating them

Empty and clean appliances before packing. Allow them to dry. Check the manufacturer's instructions for anything with a transport lock, removable reservoir, or part that needs special handling.

Put loose parts in a labeled bag or container with the appliance. A photograph of a mixer or coffee machine before disassembly can save a search for the correct attachment later. Secure cords so they do not catch on nearby items.

Use the original packaging if it is still sound and fits the appliance. Otherwise, use suitable cushioning and a box that supports its weight. Tell the mover about oversized or unusually heavy appliances when the inventory is prepared; do not assume every appliance can be handled like a countertop toaster.

## Leave a usable kitchen for the first evening

Pack a clearly labeled first-use box with a few plates, cups, utensils, dish soap, a cloth, and the small items you will want before the rest of the cupboards are unpacked. Keep that box distinct from the sealed general kitchen boxes.

At the new address, check boxes for visible damage as they arrive. Unpack onto a clear surface. Flatten wrapping as you go so a small lid or attachment is not thrown away inside it.
